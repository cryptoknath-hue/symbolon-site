Where this actually stands

The public testnet is not open yet. The seed list is empty, no block has been mined on a shared chain, and the network page on this site shows a single node with a height of zero. That is the honest state, stated here rather than discovered after an hour of setup.

What is ready: the software, the merge-mining integration against a real P2Pool, and the whole service-node mechanism — registration, proof of service, auditor probes, absence reports, epoch payouts. All of it runs today against a local chain, and every step below works right now on your own machine.

What is missing is the part nobody can do alone: a pool building Monero testnet templates, and a handful of reachable nodes. Both are in progress. When seed nodes exist they will be published in params.seeds and announced here; until then the "connect to the network" step is the only one you cannot do.

What you can do today

Run the whole thing on a local chain, and break it. That is more useful to this project right now than waiting.

A node, a wallet, a block

You need Python 3.12 and the cryptography package. Nothing else — no compiler, no database, no build step.

# a local chain with a fake proof of work: no monerod required
python3 -m tessera --network local --pow sha256 --data-dir /tmp/tsr

# in another terminal
python3 tools/wallet.py new
python3 tools/wallet.py address
python3 tools/fake_p2pool.py http://127.0.0.1:19081 <your tsrl address> 5
python3 tools/wallet.py balance

fake_p2pool.py plays P2Pool's side of the three merge-mining RPC calls, with a fake proof of work. Blocks arrive in seconds.

Back up ~/.tessera/wallet.json now. It holds your private key in the clear, it is not encrypted, and it cannot be regenerated.

The real path — monerod, P2Pool, your miner

This is merge mining as it actually works: your miner looks for one RandomX hash, and it settles both chains. You do not split your hashrate and you do not spend an extra hash.

monerod --testnet --non-interactive \
        --rpc-bind-ip 127.0.0.1 --rpc-bind-port 28081 \
        --zmq-pub tcp://127.0.0.1:28083 --disable-rpc-ban

python3 -m tessera --network testnet --monerod http://127.0.0.1:28081

./p2pool --host 127.0.0.1 --rpc-port 28081 --zmq-port 28083 \
         --wallet <XMR testnet address> --stratum 127.0.0.1:3333 \
         --merge-mine 127.0.0.1:19081 <your tsrt address>

xmrig -o 127.0.0.1:3333 -u <XMR testnet address>

The Monero RPC must be unrestricted, because Tessera verifies RandomX through calc_pow, which a restricted RPC does not expose. Keep it on localhost.

P2Pool waits for a fully synced daemon before it starts, repeating monerod is busy syncing until then. The Monero testnet is about three million blocks: count a night for the first sync.

A service node on Tor

This is the part that matters most and the part that has been exercised least. The chain pays for audited availability of Tor-only Monero nodes, and until real hidden services have registered, probed each other and been paid across several epochs, that premise is only tested against synthetic data.

The full procedure is on its own page: Run a service node.

Exercise the service layer without mining

You can run the whole registry-and-audit mechanism on a local chain, while still deriving challenges from real Monero blocks:

python3 -m tessera --network local --pow sha256 \
        --monero-blocks http://127.0.0.1:28081 --data-dir /tmp/tsr

The proof of work stays fake, so blocks are cheap; the service challenges are computed against a real monerod, so proofs and probes are the real thing. Two wallets, two hidden services, and you have a complete audit loop on one machine.

When the testnet opens

Two commands change, and only two.

# your node finds the network from the seed list, then from its own address book
python3 -m tessera --network testnet --monerod http://127.0.0.1:28081

# or point it at specific peers
python3 -m tessera --network testnet --peers <host>:19082,<host>:19082

The seed list ships in params.seeds. It is deliberately outside the consensus digest: adding an entry point is not a fork, and nobody has to upgrade because a new node appeared.

Your node refuses a peer whose network, chain_id, genesis or consensus parameters differ. That last check exists because two nodes can share a genesis and still disagree on emission or epoch length — a disagreement that otherwise only surfaces when they split on a block.

curl -s http://127.0.0.1:19081 -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":"0","method":"get_info"}' \
     -H 'Content-Type: application/json'

The genesis and params_digest in that answer are what two nodes must agree on. If yours differ from the published ones, you are not on this chain.

What will not work, and is meant not to

No clearnet endpoint can register. Consensus accepts .onion v3 and .b32.i2p and nothing else. Subsidising clearnet would fund exactly what Monero defends against.

Nothing is paid per request. Probes check that a node answers. Paying per request would create a direct incentive to log, classify and attribute user traffic, which is the opposite of the point.

There is no light client yet. Blocks now commit to their transactions through a merkle tree and the node serves inclusion proofs (get_tx_proof), so one can be written — but it has not been.

Coins are worth nothing and are meant to be. There is no market, no premine, no presale, no sale of any kind, and nothing here is an offer of anything.
